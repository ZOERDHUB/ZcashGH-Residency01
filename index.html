A field guide for people new to Zcash

How the shielded network actually fits together

Zcash lets you send money the way you'd hand someone cash — privately. This guide walks through where the technology came from, how a payment travels from your wallet to the network, which software keeps the ledger honest, and how to read a Zcash address on sight.

Reading the flow

  • Downstream (node → indexer → wallet): the full node validates and stores every block. The indexer repackages that history into a lightweight stream a phone can handle. The wallet pulls that stream and uses its own keys, locally, to work out which incoming transactions belong to it.
  • Upstream (wallet → indexer → node): when you send funds, your wallet builds and signs the transaction itself, then hands it to the indexer, which forwards it to a full node to be checked against consensus rules and broadcast to the peer-to-peer network.
  • Why split the job at all: a full node's only concern is enforcing consensus correctly. An indexer's only concern is serving that data efficiently. Because the split is clean, a bug or slowdown in the indexer layer never puts the ledger itself at risk.

Zebra vs. Zakura

The original zcashd node reached end of life in 2026. Its replacement is Zebra, the Zcash Foundation's from-scratch implementation — and Zakura, a newer independent fork of Zebra built for much higher throughput. Both speak the same consensus rules, so they interoperate on the same network.

Why run a full node at all, on either implementation

  • You verify the entire chain yourself instead of trusting someone else's copy of it.
  • You can run your own light-client indexer and wallet backend without depending on a third party.
  • You strengthen the peer-to-peer network's resilience and decentralization simply by relaying blocks.
  • It's a prerequisite for mining infrastructure and for services like block explorers.
